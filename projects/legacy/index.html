Overview

I have rebuilt the entire end-user interface with our LastKit design system’s components while keeping the codebase aligned with both existing and future solutions. Our users desired a refresh in our UI, and we needed to act on it as quickly as possible. Prebuilt components from our design system allowed us to move fast and clean while executing this project.

  • Higher User Engagement
  • Faster Task Completion
  • Increasing NPS

The problem

We realized that the intent and usability behind many of our flows had become muddled.

The information architecture had grown from years of layering features without a clear structure.

“Some days, this product seems like a version 0.9 release.”
“I often get frustrated that I can’t edit or generate a password directly when adding or saving a new entry, whether I’m on a webpage or in the Vault. It makes the product feel incomplete, and it doesn’t seem like the feedback surveys lead to any real improvements.”
“After updating the Mac app, the new UI feels awkward and inefficient, especially since the vault no longer shows quick ‘Copy Password’ or ‘Copy Username’ options when hovering over entries.”

So we decided to start with the part people rely on the most: the browser extension and the Vault.

Research & Discovery

Our research approach combined user interviews, rapid testing, and the Double Diamond framework to uncover problems and validate solutions quickly. This helped us understand real user needs while iterating efficiently toward a clearer, more effective direction.

User interviews

We began with thorough research including user interviews, desk studies, support and sales insights, community feedback, and data analysis.

This revealed major gaps in usability, flow, and overall structure.

Sketching & Ideation

Our customers told us that many of our interfaces felt outdated and difficult to use. We also evaluated and measured usability and usefulness at several key points to inform our direction.

The software has accumulated significant legacy and layered UI/UX over the years, resulting in a structure that’s become cluttered and unintuitive.

The solution

I wanted to enable the developers to update the UI without any major technical blockers from other pipelines and roadmap items. The solution is changing the UI gradually from the legacy to what is defined in the final designs. With the help of this method, we were able to provide a new, clean and better user experience to our users, while supporting our engineering teams with future-proof and easy-to-maintain code.

Information Architecture (IA)

Better IA, more focus on content, and smoother navigation, all paired with a more consistent overall look.

  • Better User Experience
  • Higher Efficiency & Productivity

Standardised UI

It has a more standard and modern appearance, with a cleaner, lighter design that makes it feel simpler and easier to use. The overall look is more streamlined and visually appealing, creating a clearer and more user-friendly interface.

Impact & Results

Our users showed their gratitude towards the new and refreshed UI. They can now easily use their Vault without any restrictions caused by the old design.

  • User Interface
  • User flow

  • Look ’n Feel

Impact 1: Brand = Trust

By introducing a modern look and feel on top of stronger foundations, we can help LastPass rebuild trust and move past the lingering “security incident” perception.

Impact 2: Consistency = Confidence

Bringing all interfaces into alignment makes LastPass instantly recognizable, reassuring users that they’re using the real product and not a suspicious imitation.

Impact 3: Design System

The Design System is central to creating this unified experience, so it received significant updates and improvements as part of the process.
